The problem

Machines and AI are starting to act on their own.

Most can't prove who allowed it. Rules live in documents, not inside the systems. When something goes wrong, the logs show what happened, but not who said yes or why.

01

Nobody can prove who said yes

A machine or AI can act without a clear, written permission that has an end date.

02

Things change without anyone noticing

Software, settings and AI models get swapped out without anyone signing off.

03

The record has gaps

Ordinary logs can be edited, lost, or missing the one detail an investigator needs.

The idea

A shared workshop for real systems.

Every product is a builder. The builders borrow the same tools, a guard checks every use, and a logbook writes it all down. Make a tool once, and every product gets it.

  1. The shared toolboxAI, design tools, memory and simulation, made once and reused by every product.
  2. The limitsThe lines each machine and product has to stay inside, written where the system can check them.
  3. The permissionsWho may do what, where, and until when. Every permission has an owner and an end date.
  4. The double-checkKeeps confirming that software, settings and AI versions still match what was approved.
  5. The plug-in kitsSimple kits so any software, robot or sensor can connect without being rewritten.
  6. The logbookA signed record of every decision, ready for a review, an audit or an investigation.

Try it first

Test the future before it happens.

A digital twin is a virtual copy of a real system. USG Radius can try a proposed action on the copy first and show what might go wrong. The copy gives advice. The permission check still makes the call.

01

Proposed action

02

Simulated outcomes

The copy plays out the possible paths and flags any that cross a limit.

Checked before it runs

Only then does the real request go to the permission check.
